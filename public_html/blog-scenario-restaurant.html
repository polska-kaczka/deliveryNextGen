An old-town restaurant — evening rush without couriers

Around 800 deliveries a month, most between 7 and 10 pm, addresses inside a pedestrian zone. What a Growth plan could look like for a restaurant like this.

17 November 2026 · 4 min read

Example scenario. This page describes a typical implementation based on our published prices and process. It is not a description of a real customer, and the figures are illustrative.

Starting point

A restaurant in a historic centre. Around 800 delivery orders a month, with a clear evening peak. Many addresses are inside a pedestrian zone that cars can’t enter, so couriers leave their scooters at the edge and finish on foot.

Choosing a plan

At this volume the Growth plan fits: up to 1,000 deliveries a month, 3 robots, restaurant mode and a priority queue. At €799 net per month and 800 deliveries, that’s about €1.00 net per delivery. Work out your own volume in the pricing calculator.

What an evening looks like

  1. The order from the POS reaches the platform through the API in under a second.
  2. The dispatcher assigns the nearest free robot. The kitchen sees in the dashboard which robot is coming and when.
  3. The dish goes into the insulated compartment, which keeps the temperature.
  4. The customer gets a tracking link and a PIN. The robot takes lanes a scooter can’t enter.
  5. After pick-up the robot returns to the restaurant or — if the queue is empty — to charge.

What to measure

  • time from kitchen hand-off to customer pick-up during the rush,
  • food temperature and quality in customer reviews,
  • cost per delivery compared with today.

Things to watch

At first, keep orders from distant districts on your existing channel. Robots perform best on short, dense routes — that’s also where the effect shows fastest.
