A pilot should answer one question: will robots work for your orders, on your streets? To get a reliable answer, prepare a few things.

1. Choose the area

Start with one location and the area where deliveries are hardest today: a pedestrian zone, an old town, a gated estate. A small, dense area produces plenty of data quickly.

2. Gather order data

  • how many orders per day and at what times,
  • typical distance from the pick-up point,
  • type of cargo (hot food, parcels, groceries).

This is what we use to choose the plan and the number of robots.

3. Decide how orders reach us

To start, the no-code dashboard or a CSV import is enough. Once the pilot proves itself, move to the REST API with webhooks — most integrations are live in about 48 hours.

4. Prepare the pick-up point

Who puts the order into the robot, and where? One fixed spot near the exit and a person who knows how to confirm loading is all it takes.

5. Tell your customers

Customers get a tracking link and a one-time PIN. Add one sentence to the order confirmation: “A robot will bring your order — you’ll get the PIN by text.”

6. Measure from day one

MetricWhy
Order-to-delivery timeIs the customer served faster than today
First-attempt success rateHow many deliveries succeed first time
Cost per deliveryComparison with your current model
Customer feedbackIs collecting from a robot convenient

7. Agree a checkpoint

After a few weeks we sit down with the data and decide: scale up, change the area or the hours. No commitment before that decision.
