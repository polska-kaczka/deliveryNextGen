Starting point

An operator of a large site — a university campus, a business park or a gated estate. Courier parcels end up at reception or the gatehouse, and recipients have to collect them in person. At peak times there’s a queue.

Choosing a plan

A rollout like this usually calls for the Enterprise plan: a fleet sized for the site, custom integrations (for example with the reception system), SLA guarantees and an on-site deployment team. Pricing is agreed individually.

How it works

  1. Couriers drop parcels at a single point by the gate.
  2. Reception scans the parcel and the system creates a robot delivery.
  3. The robot follows a fixed route to the building, dormitory or house.
  4. The recipient gets a notification and a PIN and collects the parcel at the entrance.

Why it’s good ground for robots

Short distances, predictable routes and foot traffic instead of cars. And the operator can set its own rules for robots on its land.

What to measure

  • time from the parcel reaching the gate to collection,
  • reception workload at peak times,
  • satisfaction of the people using the site.
